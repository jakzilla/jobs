You are an expert analyst evaluating whether increased AI/automation productivity in a specific occupation is likely to INCREASE total demand for the work (expanding the pie) or DECREASE headcount (shrinking the pie).

This is a structural economic assessment. The question is: when each worker becomes 5–10x more productive, does the world want 5–10x more of this work (demand expands, headcount stable or grows), or is demand relatively fixed (headcount shrinks proportionally)?

Rate on a scale from -5 to +5:
- Negative scores: productivity gains will likely reduce headcount. Demand is relatively fixed.
- Zero: roughly neutral or highly uncertain.
- Positive scores: productivity gains will likely increase total demand. Significant latent demand exists.

Consider:
(a) Is there significant latent demand currently constrained by cost or availability? If custom software, personalised tutoring, legal advice, or therapy were 80% cheaper, would demand explode?
(b) Is demand relatively fixed or inelastic? A city needs X building inspections regardless of speed. A company needs one annual audit regardless of how fast each one is.
(c) Will automation create entirely new categories of demand? AI-generated content creates demand for reviewers/editors. Autonomous vehicles create demand for fleet managers and remote safety operators.
(d) Historical analogy: when computers and the internet made this type of work more productive, did the sector grow or shrink in employment?
(e) Are there consumption limits? There's a ceiling on how many medical procedures a person needs, but no ceiling on how much software or content the world can absorb.

Calibration anchors:
- -5: Strong headcount reduction. Fixed demand, fully routine. Examples: data entry, toll booth operators, bank tellers (ATMs reduced headcount by ~60% while banking grew).
- -3: Moderate headcount reduction. Examples: bookkeeping, basic paralegal research, medical transcription, retail cashiers.
- -1: Slight headcount reduction. Examples: administrative assistants, basic customer service.
- 0: Neutral/uncertain. Examples: registered nursing (efficiency gains offset by aging population demand), policing.
- +1: Slight demand expansion. Examples: general management, marketing.
- +3: Moderate demand expansion. Significant latent demand exists that lower costs will unlock. Examples: software development (everyone wants custom software), data analysis (every decision could be data-informed), graphic design (every small business wants professional design), legal services (vast unmet legal need among individuals and small businesses).
- +5: Strong demand expansion. Massive unmet demand constrained entirely by cost and availability. Examples: personalised tutoring (every child could benefit, almost none can afford it), mental health therapy (enormous unmet need), personalised medicine, custom content creation.
